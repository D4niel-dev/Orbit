Features

Everything you need to chat privately

No accounts, no signup server, no conversation logs in the cloud. Just you and the people on your network.

Peer-to-peer

Messages travel directly between devices on the LAN. No intermediary servers and no central point of failure.

End-to-end encrypted

Direct messages use ECDH key exchange and AES-256-GCM, with one key scheme across desktop and Android — an encrypted conversation works in both directions between any two Orbit devices.

File sharing

Send photos, documents, and large files over P2P. Transfers stay on your network — no cloud upload, no compression applied today.

Group chats

Create groups with roles, invite codes, and member management. Collaborate with everyone on the same network.

Privacy mode

Optional session-only attachment storage. Keep presence, receipts, and media under your control.

Cross-platform

Electron desktop on Windows, macOS, and Linux, plus Capacitor Android. One protocol, one identity system, and one encryption scheme across both — desktop and phone talk to each other properly.

Pair by QR code

Show a QR code, scan it from the other device, and you're connected — identity, address, and encryption key all in one. No typing IP addresses.

New in

Voice & video calls

Call someone straight from a chat on desktop or Android — accept or decline, mute, toggle the camera, and see how long the last call lasted right in the conversation. Group calls are desktop-only for now. Everything runs peer-to-peer over WebRTC with no relay server.

Threads & replies

Reply to any message and the chain stays together — indented with a reply count, and it survives restarts. Open a thread to read it end to end and jump back to the original.

Notifications

Orbit tells you when someone messages, mentions you or calls — even when it is closed. Each kind of notification carries its own icon and can be set to its own level, so a call can be loud while background work stays silent.

Waits for people who are offline

Send to someone who is not reachable and the message waits instead of vanishing. On a local network people come and go constantly — a laptop closes, a phone leaves Wi-Fi range — so Orbit holds it and delivers the moment they are back.

Back up everything

The Local Vault saves your messages, attachments and settings to one encrypted file on your own device — and writes it in chunks, so even a large history backs up without running out of memory. Save a copy to Downloads or share it, and delete old ones when you are done.

Get started

How it works

No account creation. No configuration. Install it, join a network, and start talking.

You'll need
  • Two devices on the same Wi-Fi or LAN.
  • Orbit installed on each — grab the installers in the download section.
  • Nothing else — nearby peers appear on the network automatically.
  1. 01

    Install Orbit on each device

    Download the beta build for your platform from GitHub Releases — Windows, macOS, Linux, or Android. Installers are unsigned; every release ships a GPG-signed SHA256SUMS.

  2. 02

    Put both devices on the same network

    Connect to the same Wi-Fi or LAN. Orbit finds nearby clients automatically — no IP entry or account setup required.

  3. 03

    Start a chat — no account needed

    Open the app, pick a display name, and start messaging. Files and groups work the same way — traffic stays on your network even if the internet is down.

Orbit is beta software and LAN-only today — see the FAQ or GitHub issues for what's next.

Architecture

Built for speed and privacy

A readable stack — Electron, Capacitor, SQLite — designed so you can inspect every hop.

Encryption
ECDH key agreement with HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM, using a single key format on desktop and Android — so a phone and a laptop can hold an encrypted conversation with each other.
Discovery
UDP beacon broadcasts on port 45678 — automatic peer discovery, direct LAN sockets, no relay.
Integrity
SHA-256 hash computed on every file transfer, verified on both ends.
Storage
Local SQLite database with export/restore backups.
Platform
Electron desktop + Capacitor Android — one shared codebase and protocol.

The protocol is in the open — read it in shared/network/protocol.js.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Orbit works over your local network. As long as devices can reach each other on the LAN, messages, files, and calls keep flowing even when the internet is down.

Do I need an account or signup?

No accounts, no passwords, no signup server. Install Orbit, join a network, and pick a display name. Your identity and keys live on your device.

Is my data really encrypted?

Direct messages use ECDH key exchange with AES-256-GCM, and desktop and Android share the same key scheme — so an encrypted conversation works in both directions between a phone and a computer. The protocol is open source, so you can audit exactly what happens to your data.

Can I use Orbit over the internet?

Orbit is designed for local networks first. Devices need a route to reach each other; over the internet that typically means the same VPN or LAN, not a public server.

How do I add friends?

Orbit discovers nearby peers automatically on the same network. You can also add someone by IP address or share your QR code.

Can I make voice and video calls?

Yes. Open a chat and choose Voice Call or Video Call — the other person gets a ring they can accept or decline, and the call runs peer-to-peer over WebRTC with no relay server. Calls work between a phone and a computer, and every finished call is logged in the conversation so you can see how long it lasted and call back in one tap. Group calls are desktop-only for now.

Is Orbit open source?

Yes. MIT licensed, with the full source on GitHub. Read the protocol, audit the crypto, or fork it.
